Components

The Enamel components, in components/enamel-components.css. Link it after tokens/enamel.css. The picker dialog and the toast also use components/enamel-components.js for their behaviour; they look right without it. State comes from standard attributes (aria-selected, aria-pressed, aria-current, disabled), and focus shows on keyboard focus or on data-focused="true". A press from a controller is data-pressed="true".

The examples are shown side by side to compare states, so several look focused at once. In an app, only one element shows focus at a time. The examples move as they would in an app; the Motion page has demos you can operate.

Hint bar

The black bar along the bottom of the screen that shows which controller button does what.

Motion: none. The hint bar never moves.

  <span class="en-hint"><span class="en-glyph">A</span>Resume</span>
  <span class="en-hint"><span class="en-glyph">X</span>Remove</span>
  <span class="en-hint"><span class="en-glyph">B</span>Quit</span>
</div>

  <span class="en-hint"><span class="en-glyph en-glyph--shoulder">R1</span>TV shows</span>
</div>

Tabs

Pill tabs that switch between the main lists of a screen, such as Movies and TV shows.

Motion: the selected pill's background and text colour fade over --en-motion-medium; a new dot fades in over --en-motion-medium.

On a controller, tabs are switched with L1 and R1 and never show focus. For keyboard use only, a tab shows the accent ring.

<div class="en-tabs" role="tablist">
  <button class="en-tab" role="tab" aria-selected="true">Movies <span class="en-tab__count">3</span></button>
  <button class="en-tab" role="tab" aria-selected="false">TV shows <span class="en-tab__count">1</span><span class="en-dot" role="img" aria-label="New"></span></button>
</div>

List row

One item in a vertical list, such as a film or a setting, with optional details after the title.

Motion: on focus the background and text colours fade over --en-motion-fast, and back again when focus leaves.

<ul class="en-rows">
  <li><button class="en-row">Orchard Weather <span class="en-row__meta">2 parts</span></button></li>
  <li><button class="en-row" data-focused="true">Copper Tide Rising <span class="en-row__meta">1995</span></button></li>
  <li><button class="en-row">Driftwood <span class="en-row__meta">Episode 5</span></button></li>
</ul>

Tile

A video on a shelf: a paused frame, how far you have watched, and its title.

Motion: on focus the tile grows to --en-focus-scale over --en-motion-slow, without moving its neighbours, and the ring appears thick and narrows over --en-motion-ring; the ring then breathes --en-pulse-count times and rests. Losing focus reverses over --en-motion-fast.

Button

Something you press to do an action. The primary button is the main action on a screen.

Motion: when focus arrives the face rises --en-lift over --en-motion-medium with --en-ease-standard as its underside appears, and settles back over --en-motion-fast when focus leaves. Pressed, it drops onto its underside over --en-motion-fast and darkens; release springs it back over --en-motion-medium. In reduced motion focus switches at once and a press only darkens.

Buttons sit on a row. The primary button is the accent colour until it is focused; focused, every button is cream on its accent underside. On a controller, set data-pressed="true" while A is held, since a stylesheet can't see a controller's press; without it, only that movement is missing.

<button class="en-button">Rescan library</button>

<button class="en-button en-button--primary">Scan my folders</button>

<button class="en-button en-button--icon" aria-label="More">&#8943;</button>

<button class="en-button" disabled>Remove</button>

Toggle

An on or off switch for a setting. It is a button that says whether it is pressed.

Motion: the thumb slides and the track colour fades over --en-motion-medium when it is switched. As in Gaming Mode, focus appears at once, without breathing.

<div class="en-row">
  Keep screen awake
  <button class="en-toggle" aria-pressed="true" aria-label="Keep screen awake"></button>
</div>

Progress

How far something has got, such as a library scan. Set --value to a percentage.

Motion: the fill glides to each new value over --en-motion-slow.

<div>
  <div class="en-progress__head"><span>40%</span><span>Scanning</span></div>
  <div class="en-progress" role="progressbar" aria-label="Scan my folders" aria-valuenow="40" style="--value: 40%"><span class="en-progress__fill"></span></div>
</div>

Side menu

The list of places down the left of a screen. The place you are in is marked as current.

Motion: on focus the focus bar grows from the centre and the colours fade over --en-motion-medium; the current item's glow fades in and out over --en-motion-medium.

This style guide's own left menu is built from this component.

<ul class="en-menu">
  <li><a class="en-menu__item" href="#menu" aria-current="page">Movies</a></li>
  <li><a class="en-menu__item" href="#menu" data-focused="true">TV shows</a></li>
  <li><a class="en-menu__item" href="#menu">Scan my folders</a></li>
</ul>

Action bar

A bar across the top of a screen with its buttons on the right. It stays docked and opaque while the content scrolls underneath. When its buttons don't fit on one line, they wrap onto another and the bar gets taller.

Motion: none of its own. Its buttons behave like every other button.

It uses the system's buttons. An icon button always has an accessible name (aria-label). Icons are drawn as inline SVG with stroke="currentColor", so they turn dark on the cream focus fill.

<header class="en-actionbar">
  <button class="en-button">Menu</button>
  <button class="en-button en-button--icon" aria-label="Search"><svg class="en-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" aria-hidden="true"><circle cx="10.5" cy="10.5" r="6.5"/><path d="M15.5 15.5 20 20"/></svg></button>
</header>

Picker dialog

A list of choices over a darkened, blurred screen: actions to take, a setting to choose, or a question to confirm. It is as wide as its longest row, between a quarter and two thirds of the space it is given; longer text wraps.

Motion: when it opens, the darkened, blurred page appears at once and the panel fades in over --en-motion-dialog; it leaves the same way. The focused row rises and presses as a button does.

Inside a picker the focused row behaves like a button: cream with dark text, on its accent underside; rows on a page lift to the control grey instead. enamel-components.js opens it, keeps focus inside, moves with Up and Down (stopping at the ends), selects with Enter or a tap, and cancels with Escape or the Cancel row.

<dialog class="en-dialog" open>
  <div class="en-picker">
    <h2 class="en-picker__title">Remove 2 missing items?</h2>
    <div class="en-picker__rows">
      <button class="en-picker__row" value="remove"><span>Yes, remove</span><span class="en-picker__desc">Only Kettle&#x27;s list changes. Your files are never touched.</span></button>
    </div>
    <button class="en-picker__row en-picker__cancel" data-focused="true"><span>Cancel</span></button>
  </div>
</dialog>

  <span class="en-hint"><span class="en-glyph">A</span>Select</span>
  <span class="en-hint"><span class="en-glyph">B</span>Cancel</span>

Toast

A short message at the bottom right, just above the hint bar. It is as wide as its text, up to a limit. It stays three seconds after its last update, then leaves. A new one replaces the old.

Motion: it slides in from the right edge over --en-motion-slow with --en-ease-enter, and fades out where it is over the same time with the same curve. In reduced motion it simply appears and disappears.

It never takes focus and never blocks what is under it. role="status" makes screen readers read it politely. enamel-components.js shows, updates and hides it.

Step label

A small, muted line above a page title that says where you are in a short flow, such as Step 1 of 3.

Motion: none.

Put it inside the title's heading element, so screen readers read the step and the title together as one heading.

<h1><span class="en-steplabel">Step 1 of 3</span> Which folder has your movies?</h1>

Group band

The header of a group of list rows: an icon, the group's name in capitals and how many it holds.

Motion: none. A band never takes focus.

It never takes focus. Use a heading element. The two kinds differ by fill and by icon, never by colour alone, so two bands next to each other are easy to tell apart: the filled band with the drive for storage on the device itself, the outlined band with the card for storage you can take out. The icons are Enamel's own.

Badge

A small, muted tag: a label on a row, such as the folder you already chose, or after a tab's label.

Motion: none of its own. On a row it changes colour with the row, over --en-motion-fast.

Only its colours change when its row is focused or its tab is selected, so nothing changes size. Its text is part of the row's or tab's accessible name.

<div class="en-rows"><button class="en-row">SD card › Films <span class="en-badge">Movies</span></button></div>

<div class="en-tabs" role="tablist"><button class="en-tab" role="tab" aria-selected="false">Library <span class="en-badge">Beta</span></button></div>

Message in a row

A line inside a list row, in the error style, that says why this row can't be chosen.

Motion: it fades in over --en-motion-fast when it appears. In reduced motion it simply appears.

Keep the message element in the row, empty, and put the icon and text in it when it should show: role="status" makes screen readers read it politely when it appears. While it is empty it takes no space. It may make its row taller, never wider. The text is white and the icon a red disc with a dark mark, since red text is too faint on the greys.

<div class="en-rows"><button class="en-row">SD card › Films <span class="en-row__meta">62 videos</span><span class="en-row__message" role="status" aria-live="polite"><svg class="en-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" aria-hidden="true"><circle cx="12" cy="12" r="10" fill="currentColor" stroke="none"/><path d="M12 7v6"/><path d="M12 16.5v.5"/></svg>Already your movies folder. Pick a different one.</span></button></div>

Destructive row

A list row or picker row for an action that closes, removes or deletes, such as Quit or Remove: a plain row until it has the focus, then a red face with white text, and an optional icon in front.

Motion: when focus arrives the face turns red at once and rises --en-lift over --en-motion-medium as its accent underside appears, and settles back over --en-motion-fast when focus leaves, plain again at once. Pressed, it drops onto its underside over --en-motion-fast. In reduced motion focus and presses show at once, without the movement.

Add the destructive class to the row; nothing else about the row changes. Use it for those actions only: red is a status colour like error, never a second accent. Unfocused it looks exactly like the rows around it. Focused, its face turns red and rises onto its accent underside, in a list as in a picker, and a press drops it, still red. Its meta or description is then drawn bold, since white on red is just under 4.5:1 and reads well only large or bold.

Numbered steps

A short list of two to five steps, each with its number in a round mark.

Motion: none.

Use an ordered list. When a step wraps, its text lines up after its number.

<ol class="en-steps">
  <li>Make one folder for movies and one for TV shows, on this device or on a card.</li>
  <li>Copy your videos in. Give each TV show its own folder.</li>
  <li>If they are on a card, put it in. Then choose Look again.</li>
</ol>

Stat panel

A raised panel with one or two counts, each a large number with its word, and under them the folder they refer to. A stat pair puts two panels side by side.

Motion: none. A stat panel never takes focus.

It never takes focus and has no hover or focus state. role="img" and an aria-label make it one item for screen readers, so write its whole text in the label. A pair stacks when its panels cannot both fit at their minimum width. A "Not set" panel's second line says which kind it is, so two of them can be told apart.

Note

One plain line of neutral information under other content. Several can stack.

Motion: none. A note never takes focus.

It never blocks anything, never takes focus and never goes inside a row; a message in a row is for that, in the error style. The icon is optional and Enamel's own.

<p class="en-note">12 files in Films look like episodes.</p>

Page speckle

Very faint flecks on the page ground, with the occasional one in the accent colour. Put it on an empty area of the page.

Motion: none. Nothing animates.

Never put it behind list text, and nothing you can select is ever patterned. Text next to it still passes its contrast check.

<div class="en-speckle" style="min-height: 15rem"></div>

Lighter fallback

One switch for devices that stutter: data-en-lighter="true" on the page's root drops the soft shadows and the lift and press movement. Colours, cream focus, the accent underside and the speckle all stay.

Motion: faces no longer rise or drop. Everything else moves as usual.

Compare the two below, or use the Look switch at the bottom of the menu to see the whole guide this way.
